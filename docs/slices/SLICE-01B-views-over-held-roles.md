## 1 Outcome

The control in the top bar stops being a way to become another person and
becomes a way to change altitude within the authority the signed-in person
already holds, and every capability the client renders is one the server
computed.

---

## 2 Functional purpose

A user comes to this control because one person legitimately works at more than
one altitude. FRD §4.3 separates the three ideas the control confuses. A person
is accountable and is named in the trail. A role is a capability set, and several
people hold the same one: Anjali, Vikram, Priya and Arvind are all Compliance
Managers doing very different jobs. A persona is the point of view a user adopts,
deciding what the sidebar offers, what the queue contains and which approvals
appear.

FRD §4.2 states the requirement the prototype does not meet: a person may hold
several roles, person to role is many to many, and a person's queue and
navigation are the union of their roles at the altitude they select. Meera
Krishnan is Executive and Risk Committee Chair. Sunita Menon is Auditor and Audit
Committee Chair. Those are two altitudes of one person's own authority, and
moving between them is legitimate. What is not legitimate is what the prototype
does: `PERSONAS` in `apps/web/src/data/people.ts` offers eleven entries covering
nine different people, and selecting one replaces both the acting person and the
acting role in browser memory. FRD §23 D-12 calls this out, and FRD §4.3 states
the rule it breaks: a persona switch must never confer access a person does not
have.

D-045 settles the shape. The control lists only the views the signed-in person's
own roles give them, which for most people is one entry and therefore no control
at all. The two committee-chair entries survive. The Company Secretary gains his
own view, because he holds clause authority and a statutory calendar of his own
and FRD §4.3 records his absence as an open question rather than a design. That
gives ten possible views over nine roles, and any one person sees one or two.

The second half of the slice is the other side of the same rule. FRD G-03 and
`BR-AUT-01` require that authority is enforced on the server and that no screen
performs its own role comparison. D-016 records it as decided. The prototype
carries `canAct()` in `apps/web/src/lib/gating.ts` and the navigation gate in
`nav-config.ts`, both comparing roles in the browser. Under FLR-03 those become
affordances over capabilities the server computed through R-002, never the
control itself. ER-003 is conformance under D-043 and is built here with no
further approval.
