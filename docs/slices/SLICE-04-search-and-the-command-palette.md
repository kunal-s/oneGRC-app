## 1 Outcome

Command search stops reading a list built in the browser at page load and asks the
server instead: it finds every record the database actually holds, matches the full
text of a statutory provision as readily as an identifier, narrows a term search to
the caller's own department while leaving any record reachable by its identifier,
and opens the real page for what it found.

---

## 2 Functional purpose

Two people come to this surface, and neither of them wants to be here. The first
has an identifier in their hand: an inspector's letter naming a duty, a colleague's
message naming a control, a row copied out of a register. FRD §8.3 states what the
surface is for in one line: a command search that jumps to any record by identifier
or title. FRD §4.5 states why it must not be scoped for that use, and states it
twice, at `BR-SCP-03` and in the boundary rule itself: the department boundary
applies to discovery, not to navigation, so a person handed a record identifier can
always open it and a cross-reference never dead-ends. `platform.md` carries the same
sentence at the foot of section 3, and [[SLICE-01C]] already wrote it into the
contract at SCR-088-031.

The second has a phrase and no identifier. They remember that something in the
Profession Tax Act deals with the employer's liability, or that a duty somewhere
mentions a treasury challan, and they want the platform to find it. That is the use
FRD G-21 names when it says production must have full-text search across records,
clauses and evidence metadata, respecting access scope. It is also the use that
makes the boundary matter: a term search that ignores the department is a better
register than the registers, and it would undo the whole of what [[SLICE-01C]]
built. R-006 in `platform.md` section 3 settles it by saying both things at once,
records by identifier and by title, scoped to the caller's access, which is why this
slice separates the two kinds of query and applies the boundary to one of them.

Name the fiction. `apps/web/src/components/CommandSearch.tsx` builds a constant
called `INDEX` once, at module load, from `WORLD`: twenty one navigation entries
plus seven groups of seed records held in the browser. Every identifier in it is a
shape D-005 abolished. Typing `CTRL-COMP-DPB-01` finds a control that does not
exist; typing `CTRL-0002`, the one control the database actually holds, finds
nothing. Typing `OBL-0011`, a duty a person created through the real workflow, finds
nothing, while `OBL-INT-INVRES-W1` opens a page rendered from an invented record.
Four of the seven record groups, incidents, policies, audits and regulatory changes,
have no table in the schema at all, and a fifth, risks, has a table with nothing in
it. Nothing in the index has ever been scoped: the
filter is one `includes()` over a label and an id, so every persona sees every row,
and the Pages group offers `Speak-up` and `Fraud Cases` to personas FRD §4.9 does
not offer them to and §4.12 exists to keep out. The plan records the whole of it in
three places: SIM-NAV-007 says the search matches identifiers and titles over the
in-memory seed only, cannot find a record created this session in another module,
and applies no access scope; FIC-27 says command search scans every record in
memory; FLR-10 says full-text search is not real. This slice makes the index real,
the matching real and the boundary real, and it registers only the kinds of record
the platform can actually open today.

### SRCH, what the index holds

| Line | Fact |
|---|---|
| SRCH-001 | One index, and one endpoint over it. R-006 is the only search read in the plan and `GET /search` is the only route that serves it. `CommandSearch.tsx`'s `INDEX` constant is deleted rather than left standing beside it, because two indexes drift and then disagree in front of the client. `platform.md` section 8's discipline, CLAUDE.md rule 5 |
| SRCH-002 | The index is a registry: one provider per kind of searchable record, each declaring the fields it matches on, the label and sub-label it renders, the group it belongs to, the route that opens it, and how its owning department is resolved. Adding a kind is adding a provider and changing nothing else. Modelled on ENG-01's own `apps/api/src/core/ladder/registry.ts`, which solves the same problem for deadlines |
| SRCH-003 | This slice registers the five kinds that have rows in the database today **and** a detail route that resolves a real identifier: `Instrument` (4 rows), `SourceProvision` (602), `SourceClause` (1), `Obligation` (4), `Control` (1). Counts read from the database on 2026-09-07 and stated so the scope of the verification is honest, not because the code depends on them |
| SRCH-004 | `Task` (4 rows) and `Evidence` (1 row) have rows and no screen that can open one. `/tasks/:id` and `/evidence/:id` both fall through to `ComingSoon` for any identifier the seed world does not hold, so a hit on either would land the caller on a not-found page. Neither is registered here. `Task` registers in [[SLICE-18]] with SCR-050, `Evidence` in [[SLICE-21]] with SCR-069. G-21's "evidence metadata" is conformance and is assigned, not dropped |
| SRCH-005 | `Risk` has a table and no rows in it, so registering it would index nothing and its detail screen still reads the seed. Incident, Issue, Exception, Policy, Audit, Working paper, Regulatory change, Third party, Campaign, Data asset, Data-subject request and every investigation entity have no table in the schema at all. Each registers one provider inside its own module's slice. This work order writes no build step for a module that does not exist |
| SRCH-006 | A restricted case never enters the index, for anybody, and is not reachable through it by identifier either. FRD §4.12 names this surface directly: the reporter's identity is not a field, so no screen, export, search index or log can leak what the platform does not hold. The registry's provider contract states the rule so that no later module registers a case provider carelessly. The case-access check itself is AUTH-G2 and belongs to [[SLICE-26]] |
| SRCH-007 | Ranking is computed on the read and never stored. No column is named for a score: `pnpm check:derived` refuses any column matching `%_score`, and the rule behind that check, not the check, is the reason |
| SRCH-008 | The one thing the index materialises is a Postgres `tsvector` per searchable table, as a `GENERATED ALWAYS AS (...) STORED` column with a GIN index over it. It holds no fact the product displays, and it is recomputed by the database on every write to its inputs, which is exactly what CON-08 requires of a materialisation. `to_tsvector` needs no extension; `pg_trgm` and `unaccent` are available in this image and are not installed, and this slice installs neither |
| SRCH-009 | Search writes nothing and logs nothing. AUD-01 is one entry per record change and a search changes no record. D-051 already refused an audit entry for marking a notification read, on the reasoning that an act which changes nothing about a record should not multiply the log, and the same reasoning governs here. Identifier lookup reaches no record that a direct link did not already reach under `BR-SCP-03`, so it grants no new access to trail |

### SRCH, what a query matches

| Line | Fact |
|---|---|
| SRCH-020 | The fields each registered kind matches on, and the route each hit opens: `Instrument` on `id`, `shortTitle`, `title`, `citation` and `authority`, opening `/sources/<id>`; `SourceProvision` on `clauseRef`, `heading` and `verbatimText`, opening `/sources/provision/<id>`; `SourceClause` on `id`, `clauseRef`, `shortTitle`, `title` and `verbatimText`, opening `/sources/clause/<id>`; `Obligation` on `id`, `shortTitle`, `title`, `regulator` and `evidenceRequirement`, opening `/obligations/<id>`; `Control` on `id`, `shortTitle`, `title` and `description`, opening `/controls/<id>` |
| SRCH-021 | An **identifier lookup** is a query whose trimmed, upper-cased text equals an indexed record's own identifier exactly. Every other query is a **term search**. The test is equality against the column, not a guess at the shape from a regular expression, so it stays correct as D-005's formats are allocated by ENG-10 |
| SRCH-022 | A source provision has no user-facing identifier: `data-model.md` section 4 records it as internal and never rendered, and the database holds a cuid. A provision is therefore never reached by identifier lookup, and its row shows its instrument and its clause reference in place of an id |
| SRCH-023 | A term search matches full text, not a prefix of a title. Prose is matched with `plainto_tsquery` against the generated `tsvector` of SRCH-008, so `employer liability` finds a provision whose text says "liability of employer". Identifiers and short titles additionally take a case-insensitive prefix match, so `OBL-00` narrows to the duties without being an exact identifier |
| SRCH-024 | Matching is case-insensitive throughout. Diacritics are not folded, because `unaccent` is not installed and installing it is not this slice's to decide |
| SRCH-025 | An empty query returns the Pages group and nothing else, exactly as the prototype does. No minimum query length is imposed: a one-character query is a term search like any other, and SCR-081-061 tells the truth about how much it matched |
| SRCH-026 | The full text of a provision is 602 rows of statutory prose that no person has yet decided binds the firm. It is indexed, because that is what a compliance officer needs to search, and it is grouped and ordered so that a proposal never sits above a decided record. CLAUDE.md rule 7, D-014's two-tier model, and DN-045's recommendation |

### SRCH, the scope rule

| Line | Fact |
|---|---|
| SRCH-030 | **An identifier lookup is navigation and is not scoped by department.** Any signed-in person who types an existing identifier gets that record and can open it. `BR-SCP-03`, FRD §4.5, `platform.md` section 3's closing rule, and SCR-088-031, which [[SLICE-01C]] already built and verified |
| SRCH-031 | **A term search is discovery and is scoped.** A caller sees only records owned within their own department; Compliance and Company Secretarial and the Administrator see all. `BR-SCP-02`, SCR-088-030, G-21, R-006 |
| SRCH-032 | The boundary is applied by `computeScope()`, the one scope resolver ENG-14 names, in the query and never by filtering a full result set after it is read. No second resolver is written, and `apps/web/src/lib/access.ts` is not consulted |
| SRCH-033 | A record's department derives from its owner and is never stored beside it. `BR-SCP-01`, and the rule `pnpm check:derived` enforces. `Obligation` resolves through `owner.department`, `Control` through `owner.department` |
| SRCH-034 | `Instrument`, `SourceProvision` and `SourceClause` carry no department today. [[SLICE-06]] owns E-14's own department model under DRV-20's exception, and until it lands those three kinds are unscoped in a term search. This work order says so rather than inventing a boundary, the same posture [[SLICE-03]] took at EXP-011 for the same reason |
| SRCH-035 | The scope in force is stated on the surface. `BR-SCP-04`: a short list must never be mistaken for an empty world. Rendered by SCR-081-090 |
| SRCH-036 | An identifier that does not exist and one the caller may not open return the same thing, so search cannot become an enumeration oracle. REF-30, `BR-SCP-08`, SCR-088-032. Under SRCH-030 the only records a caller may not open through search are the restricted cases SRCH-006 keeps out of the index entirely, which is exactly the pair REF-30 must not distinguish |
| SRCH-037 | The caller is the server's session, never a client assertion. `GET /search` takes no person parameter, and a caller who edits the request cannot widen the boundary, the property [[SLICE-01C]] established on `GET /controls` |

### The three decisions needed, in full

**DN-043. Does the department boundary apply to command search?**

The plan says two things that read as opposites until the kind of query is
separated. `BR-SCP-03`, FRD §4.5, `platform.md` section 3 and SCR-088-031 all say a
record stays reachable by direct link and by command search, so the boundary does
not apply. G-21 and R-006 both say search respects the caller's access scope, so it
does.

*Worked example.* Rajesh Iyer is a Control Owner in IT and Information Security.
`CTRL-0002` is owned by Anjali Deshmukh in Compliance and Company Secretarial. If
Rajesh types `CTRL-0002`, does he get it? If he types `professional tax`, does he
get it?

*Recommendation.* Yes to the first, no to the second. An identifier lookup is
navigation: someone handed Rajesh that identifier and the plan has already ruled,
and built, that he can open it. A term search is discovery: unscoped, it would be a
better register than every register, and it would undo the boundary [[SLICE-01C]]
built. A restricted case is outside both, under FRD §4.12. The delta this carries is
one line of new UI, the scope footer at SCR-081-090, which `BR-SCP-04` requires once
a surface is scoped at all.

*Blocking?* No. The recommendation is consistent with everything already built and
is what this work order's contract lines state.

**DN-044. What does the palette say when a term matches more than it shows?**

The prototype takes the first 24 results and says nothing about the rest. Over 24
seed records that was invisible. Over 602 provisions and a real corpus it is a list
claiming to be complete when it is not.

*Worked example.* Anjali types `tax`. 180 provisions match. She sees 24 and has no
way to know that 156 more exist, so she concludes the platform holds 24.

*Recommendation.* Show the total, in the palette, below the last group:
`Showing 24 of 180 matches. Narrow the search.` D-021 already requires every number
to carry its basis, and this is that rule applied to a count of rows. It adds one
line to an approved screen and no other change.

*Blocking?* No.

**DN-045. Do raw provisions belong in the same result list as decided records?**

D-014 splits the source model in two: a provision is every numbered thing the
extractor found, held with no workflow and no decision; a clause is a provision a
person promoted because it binds the firm. CLAUDE.md rule 7 says classification
proposes and a human disposes. There are 602 provisions and 1 clause.

*Worked example.* Deepa types `profession tax`. If provisions rank equally with
obligations, the first screen of results is statutory text nobody has decided
anything about, and the duty she was looking for is below the fold.

*Recommendation.* Index them, because searching the words of the law is the point of
holding it, and put them in their own group named `Provisions`, ordered last, below
`Pages`, `Obligations`, `Controls`, `Clauses` and `Instruments`. A proposal never
sits above a decided record, and nothing in the list implies a provision is a
tracked duty. The alternative, leaving provisions out, would mean the platform holds
the text of the law and cannot search it, which is the opposite of G-21.

*Blocking?* No.

### The two enhancement recommendations

**ER-019.** The palette clears its query every time it opens. A person who searches,
opens the wrong record and presses `Cmd+K` again starts from an empty box and retypes.
On a seed of two dozen rows that cost nothing. Over a real corpus it is the difference
between one search and five. Keeping the last query, selected so the first keystroke
replaces it, is a small change to `CommandSearch.tsx`'s open effect. The FRD does not
require it. **Unbuilt.**

**ER-020.** Under DN-043's recommendation a caller can reach a record owned outside
their own department by typing its identifier, and nothing on the row tells them so.
They open it, see a record their registers never show them, and have no way to know
why. A department name in the sub-label, or a muted marker on out-of-scope rows,
would say it. The FRD does not require it, and the scope footer at SCR-081-090
addresses only the term-search half. **Unbuilt.**
