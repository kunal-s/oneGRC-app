## 1 Outcome

The platform gives one answer, in one voice, to the four questions a screen has
to answer when it has nothing to show, and one answer to every reason a write is
refused, and it reads the time from one place in the organisation's own zone
instead of from the moment a browser tab happened to open.

## 2 Functional purpose

Three separate fictions are being removed, and they are in one slice because they
are the same fiction wearing three coats: the platform currently answers the same
question differently depending on which handler, which screen or which module you
reached it through.

**The refusals.** `platform.md` section 6 defines thirty one refusals, each with
its exact message and the rule it enforces, "defined once so they read the same
everywhere". None of them is defined once. Fifteen have a real path in the code
today and every one of the fifteen is a string typed into the handler that throws
it. The drift is already measurable and was measured on 2026-09-08 against the
running API. REF-02's catalogue text names roles; the built message names role
codes, so a Control Owner is told `clause.notApplicable requires one of
[COMPLIANCE_MGR]; you hold [CONTROL_OWNER]`. REF-03's catalogue text names a
department; the built message names the Prisma enum member, so a Data Protection
Officer is told the action `is reserved to the ComplianceAndSecretarial
department; you are in DataProtection`, two words the interface never shows her
anywhere else. Ten of the fifteen end without the full stop the catalogue gives
them. SLICE-00 recorded both defects at close-out and assigned them here; nothing
has changed since.

REF-06 is worse than drift. Its catalogue text is `This duty cannot be submitted
without evidence: <the duty's evidence requirement>.` The built message reads
`this duty cannot be submitted without evidence: the statute requires proof of
payment (BR-EVD-01)`, and "the statute requires proof of payment" is a constant.
It is not read from the duty. Every one of the five obligations in the database
has an empty `evidenceRequirement`, so the message asserts a requirement no
record holds, on any duty, for any statute. That is the defect CLAUDE.md rule 2
exists for, and it has been shipping since the task workflow was built.

REF-30 is not built at all, and SLICE-01C's close-out in `slice-plan.md` says so
in as many words: "REF-30's exact wording stays unbuilt, correctly deferred to
SLICE-05's refusal catalogue." SLICE-01C's own contract line SCR-088-032 requires
that an identifier which does not exist and one the caller may not open return the
same message, so the difference cannot be used to enumerate records. Six
detail reads were called with a fabricated identifier on 2026-09-08 and returned
six different messages in two shapes, neither of them REF-30's: `no clause
SRC-99999`, `OBL-9999`, `no instrument INST-999`, `EVD-9999`, `CTRL-9999`, `no
provision nope`. Three of the six return the identifier alone as the whole
message, which the browser then renders under the heading `Could not load from
the API`, so a person who mistypes an identifier is told the API is broken.

**The empty states.** FRD §17.4 v2.1 states the requirement directly: empty
registers are a normal and frequent condition in production, "day one before
migration, a filter that matches nothing, a module not yet deployed, a period with
genuinely nothing to report", and each of those is a different message. FIC-16
records the prototype's opposite: no table is ever empty, anywhere, as a
deliberate seeding aesthetic. SLICE-00 recorded at S00-245 that the four messages
are not built and that the wired screens carry one crude message until this slice.
That crude message is one `EmptyState` and one `ErrorNote`, both of them exported
from the middle of `apps/web/src/pages/live/SourceLibrary.tsx`, imported by every
other live page, and used for a 404 and a transport failure alike. SLICE-06 has
already written four contract lines against the gap (SCR-053-090, 091, 093, 094,
each reading `NOT IMPLEMENTED`) and a build step that carries the crude versions
through its own rebuild because losing them would leave the screen with none.
SLICE-04 did the same at SCR-081-070 and SCR-081-071 and said plainly that it was
building "a crude version, which is what the plan says to do until SLICE-05
lands".

**The clock.** `platform.md` ENG-09 is one line: every deadline, every countdown,
every age, every relative phrase, in the organisation's time zone, "Not built. The
prototype anchors everything to page load, which is the load-bearing fiction."
FRD §23 D-01 names the same anchor as the load-bearing fiction and gives the
production answer: all time flows through one clock service in the organisation's
zone (`BR-SCH-09`), and "as at" is stamped on every metric surface and pack.

Four slices in a row have recorded the dependency and gone round it. SLICE-01D's
CON-012 read the instant and the zone directly for REF-25. SLICE-02's LDR-012 did
it again for the ladder and a third time in `fmtRelative` on the bell, and its
section 8 says "Three places resolve time until SLICE-05 collapses them", marked
"note, must not become permanent". SLICE-03 recorded that it was the third slice
to read the instant directly. `apps/api/src/core/ladder/timezone.ts` carries the
dependency in its own file header. That is the queue this slice clears.

The fiction has already reached real data, which is what makes it urgent rather
than tidy. `apps/web/src/lib/time.ts` computes `NOW` once, at module load, and
`fmtRelative` measures every relative phrase against it and never against the
present. Four surfaces that read the server import it: the notifications bell in
`TopBar.tsx`, the `/notifications` page, the live obligation detail's ladder rows,
and the evidence drawer. So the bell tells a person a real notification about a
real duty arrived "3h ago", and goes on saying "3h ago" for as long as the tab
stays open. Two tabs opened ten minutes apart disagree about the same row.

The server has the same split. `computeRungs()` resolves a rung's moment through
`startOfDayInZone()` in the organisation's zone, correctly. The derived `overdue`
on `GET /obligations/:id` compares a `@db.Date` due date against a raw
`new Date()`, so an obligation cycle due on 8 September flips to overdue at
2026-09-08T00:00:00Z, which is 05:30 in Asia/Kolkata on the morning of the day it
is due, roughly eighteen hours before the ladder considers it one minute late.
Two answers, in one server, about one duty, and `data-model.md`'s own DRV-17 says
which is right: `dueDate < today`, and today is a calendar date in a time zone,
not an instant.

**DN-047. What is the fourth empty message, when nothing records whether a module

FRD §17.4 v2.1 and FIC-16 both name four cases, and the third is "a module not

**DN-048. What does REF-06 say when the duty carries no stated evidence
requirement?**

REF-06's catalogue text is `This duty cannot be submitted without evidence: <the
duty's evidence requirement>.` The built message substitutes a constant, `the
statute requires proof of payment`, which is true of no record. Every
`Obligation` row in the database has an empty `evidenceRequirement`, so removing
the constant leaves the message with nothing after its colon.

*Worked example.* Deepa submits `TSK-00011` with nothing attached. Today she is
told the statute requires proof of payment. The duty says nothing of the kind and
neither does the clause behind it.

*Recommendation.* Where the field holds a value, the message reads the catalogue's
full sentence with the value in it. Where it is empty, the message stops:
`This duty cannot be submitted without evidence.` A message that names no
requirement is honest; a message that names an invented one is the defect
CLAUDE.md rule 2 exists for. That a duty carries no stated evidence requirement
at all is a data gap belonging to whichever slice creates duties with one, not to
the refusal.

*Blocking?* No.

**DN-049. Where does the "as at" stamp go on a surface carrying more than one

FRD §23 D-01 says "as at" is stamped on every metric surface and pack.
`BR-SCH-09` says the zone is named. D-021 says every number carries its
denominator or its basis on the surface that shows it. None of the three says
whether one surface with four numbers gets one stamp or four, and the wording is
customer-facing copy on approved screens with no FRD text to cite, which is
exactly the bar DN-031 set for raising a decision rather than guessing.

*Worked example.* The Source Library, once SLICE-06 rebuilds it, carries four

basis.

*Recommendation.* One stamp per surface, at the foot, in the treatment SLICE-04

an approved screen for a line of provenance sitting under a result. Where two
reads at two instants feed one surface, that is two surfaces and it gets two
stamps.

*Blocking?* No.

### The two enhancement recommendations

**ER-022.** A refused governed action leaves no trace anywhere. Under REFU-008 a
refusal writes no record and no audit entry, which is right under AUD-01 and
D-051 because nothing changed. But nothing then records that a Control Owner
attempted a Compliance Manager's decision, and an administrator reviewing the
platform cannot see that anyone was ever refused anything. A separate refusal
log, outside the hash-chained audit log so the chain keeps its meaning, would
answer it. The FRD does not require it and AUD-01 arguably rules against putting
it in the audit log. **Unbuilt.**

**ER-023.** REF-30 deliberately cannot say whether a record exists, so a person
handed a real identifier they may not open reaches a dead end with no next step.
The platform knows who owns that record without telling the caller, so a
"request access" affordance could route the ask to the owner and say nothing
about what was asked for. Under `BR-SCP-03` most records stay reachable anyway,
so the case is narrow today and widens as case access (AUTH-G2) and any later
record-level restriction land. The FRD does not require it. **Unbuilt.**
