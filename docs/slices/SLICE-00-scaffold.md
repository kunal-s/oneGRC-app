## 1 Outcome

The machine underneath every screen is finished and honest: origin reads
`reference`, `sample` or `earned` on every record, the database itself refuses to
alter the audit log, deleting a person can never erase what they did, the sample
purge removes everything it claims to and refuses when it would orphan
provenance, the frontend reaches the server only through named functions, and
every verification command in `CLAUDE.md` passes while both applications still
look identical.

## 2 Functional purpose

Nobody visits this slice. It is the floor everything else stands on, and the FRD
names that floor precisely: "The items that cannot stay simulated in **any** real
deployment are persistence, authentication, server-side authorization, the
scheduler, evidence storage and the audit log's immutability" (FRD §19.3). Four
of those six belong here: persistence, the identity the server resolves for
itself, the one authority check, and a log that cannot be altered. The scheduler
is [[SLICE-02]] and evidence storage is [[SLICE-03]].

What the firm gets is the ability to answer three questions an inspector or an
internal auditor asks first, and to answer them about the platform itself rather
than about the data inside it. Who did this, and could the record of it have been
changed since? FRD §16 puts it plainly: the product "should be able to
demonstrate, on itself, the things it asks customers to demonstrate: who accessed
what, that changes were approved by someone other than the maker, that its own
retention rules are enforced, and that its log is complete." Which of these
records did the platform capture, and which did it inherit or invent? That is
what origin answers, and FRD §18.2 principle 3 requires it of every migrated
record so that "a year later a user can tell the difference between what the
platform captured and what it inherited." And can the demonstration data be taken
out without taking real work with it? That is the purge, and it is the difference
between a pilot that can become a production deployment and one that has to be
rebuilt.

The prototype simulates all of it. FRD §23 D-13 records that authority checks
live in the browser, and D-14 records that the audit log is append-only only
until the page is reloaded. This repository has already made most of that real:
the authority check reads one table on the server, and the log is hash-chained
and written in the same transaction as the change it records. What is left is the
part that is hardest to retrofit and cheapest now. A retention floor cannot be
applied backwards to data that has already been deleted (FRD `BR-DAT-05`). An
origin value cannot be inferred after the fact. And a foreign key that quietly
nulls a provenance field has already destroyed the thing the trail exists to hold
by the time anybody notices.

## 10 The slice loop discipline

**One screen. One session. One commit.** If this needs two sessions it was scoped
too big. Split it and say so. Parts A and B, part C, and parts D and E are the
natural split lines, and none of them touches the same file as another.

**Plan before code.** Read this document, approve or redirect, then build.

**Build only this slice's lines.** Anything else noticed goes in section 8.2.
Never built opportunistically.

**Point at the plan, never at the prototype source.** Say "implement SLICE-00
step A4, contract lines S00-021 and S00-148", not "fix the foreign keys".

**Verify by clicking, side by side, personally.** Section 7 has eighteen steps
and only three of them are commands anybody has to trust.

**Classify every difference.** Drift or delta. Nothing unclassified.

**Report, do not resolve.** Ambiguity becomes a decision needed, with an example
and a recommendation.

**Never build an unapproved enhancement.** ER-003 and ER-011 both touch this
slice's ground and both stay unbuilt.

**Close out before starting the next slice.**
