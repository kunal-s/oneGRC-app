## 1 Outcome

A deadline the platform holds now chases itself: rungs fire on time to real named
people resolved through the department-head map, each firing is delivered,
retried and confirmed, each is written to the hash-chained audit log, and a
person can open the bell, the notifications screen or the duty and see exactly
which rungs fired, when, and to whom.

---

## 2 Functional purpose

A compliance officer does not come to this surface. That is the point of it. FRD
§5.27 states the purpose in one line: nothing in the platform waits on someone
noticing. The ladder is the single engine that chases every deadline the firm
holds, and its output is the trail proving the firm chased. A user meets it three
ways: the bell tells them something is owed, the notifications screen gives them
the history behind the bell, and the record shows the chase against that one
deadline. What it accomplishes for them is narrower and more valuable than a
reminder: FRD §20.2 requirement 16 asks the firm to demonstrate an overdue duty
showing exactly which reminders and escalations fired, when, and to whom, and to
find the same events in the audit log. It passes when the firm can prove it
chased. An inspector does not accept "we would have chased".

The ladder is fixed and the same for every kind of deadline, which is what makes
it learnable: reminders to the owner at seven, three and one days before due;
escalation at one day overdue to the owner and their department head, at three
days to the Compliance Officer, at seven days to the executive (FRD §5.27,
`BR-ESC-01`). `BR-ESC-02` allows exactly one such engine, `BR-ESC-04` requires
every rung to name a real person through the department-head map of FRD §4.13,
`BR-ESC-05` keeps a filed duty quiet, and `BR-ESC-06` chases each step of a
multi-step duty on its own date to its own owner, because a three-party chain has
three accountable people and chasing only the first is chasing nobody.

Name the fiction. Nothing has ever fired. `apps/web/src/lib/reminders.ts` computes
the whole ladder in the browser against the moment the page loaded, and
`escalationSeedNotifications(3)` folds the three most recent computed escalations
into a hand-written list of four more so the bell is never empty. Every row in the
bell today is either a derivation over invented obligations or a literal written
into `SEED_NOTIFICATIONS`. The people it names come from `apps/web/src/data/people.ts`,
not from the database. No message was sent, no delivery was confirmed, no retry
was attempted, and no firing reached the audit log. SIM-NAV-006 records it,
`platform.md` FLR-04 and FLR-08 record it, and FRD §19.2 grades G-05 and G-06
Essential and names the scheduler as one of the six things that cannot stay
simulated in any real deployment. This slice makes the firing real, the recipients
real, the delivery real and the trail real, and it deliberately keeps the
derivation property G-05 asks for, so the ladder for any deadline can still be
recomputed from the deadline and the intervals and checked against what actually
fired.

## 3 Screen contract

This slice is an engine, not a screen, so the contract is written the way
[[SLICE-01C]] and [[SLICE-01D]] write theirs: the rule first, then the surfaces
that render its result.

Behaviour, fields, states and rules come from `docs/plan/` and the FRD per D-042.

### What the ladder is pointed at

| Line | Fact |
|---|---|
| LDR-030 | One engine, many deadlines. A module that needs chasing registers its own date with this engine and never builds a second one. `BR-ESC-02`, ENG-01 |
| LDR-031 | The engine reads its subjects through a registry: one provider per kind of chased thing, each returning the subject's identifier, its deadline, its owner and whether it is still active. Adding a kind is adding a provider |
| LDR-032 | **This slice registers two kinds, because two are all that have rows.** TIM-01 obligation cycle, pointed at `ObligationCycle.dueDate`. TIM-02 task, pointed at `Task.dueDate`. `workflows.md` section 5 |
| LDR-033 | TIM-01 and TIM-02 are the two rows `workflows.md` marks SIMULATED, meaning the prototype computes them and nothing fires. | LDR-034 | An obligation cycle is active while its state is `Due` or `In review`, and is not chased once it is `Filed`. `BR-ESC-05`, CYC-S3, DRV-17 |
| LDR-035 | A task is active while its state is `Open`, `In progress`, `Submitted` or `Returned`, and is not chased once it is `Done` or `Cancelled`. `BR-ESC-05`, TSK-S5, TSK-S6, DRV-17 |
| LDR-036 | A cycle and each of its tasks carry their own independent ladders, against their own due dates and to their own owners. `BR-ESC-06`, TSK-I6. A cycle with three tasks carries four ladders, not one |
| LDR-037 | A cycle's owner for ladder purposes is the owner of its obligation. A task's owner is its assignee. `workflows/duty.md` CYC-T1, TSK-T1 |
| LDR-038 | A rung whose moment has not arrived does not fire, and is not written. Only a fired rung becomes a row |
| LDR-039 | A rung whose moment passed while the platform was not running fires on the next tick after it starts. The firing records both the moment the rung was due and the moment it actually fired, and the two differing is itself part of the trail. A missed window is never silently dropped |

### Firing, and the trail

| Line | Fact |
|---|---|
| LDR-050 | The engine ticks on an interval inside the API process, with no external scheduler, no cron service and no hosted queue. CON-01 and CON-03 require an on-premise and air-gapped deployment to be possible, and a dependency on a scheduling service would remove that |
| LDR-051 | Each tick fires every rung whose moment has passed and which has not already fired, across every registered subject that is still active |
| LDR-052 | A rung fires exactly once per recipient per channel, however many times the tick runs. The uniqueness is enforced by the database, on the recipient, the event type, the subject, the rung offset and the channel, not by the engine remembering |
| LDR-053 | One rung is one transaction: the notification rows for its recipients and its one audit entry commit together or not at all. `platform.md` AUD-01, section 5. A firing that cannot be logged did not happen |
| LDR-054 | The audit entry names the system as actor, not a person. `actorId` null, `actorLabel` `system`, exactly as the sample purge already writes. `platform.md` AUD-04 |
| LDR-055 | The audit entry's action is `ladder.fire`. Its `entityType` and `entityId` are the chased subject, so the trail is navigable from the record. AUD-05 |
| LDR-056 | The audit entry's detail carries the rung's interval label, its target-role label, the named recipients, the channel each went out on, and the moment the rung was due. `BR-ESC-03` requires the firm to be able to prove it chased, which means proving who was chased |
| LDR-057 | The firing path never calls `AuthorityService.assert`. It is a system event, not a governed mutation, so it does not pass through `GovernedMutationService.run()` and REF-01 never sees it. It writes through `AuditService.append` inside its own transaction, the way `sample.purge`'s system entry already does |
| LDR-058 | The audit chain is unchanged. No new entry kind, no change to the hash, and `pnpm --filter api verify:audit` continues to walk clean |
| LDR-059 | `ladder.fire` acquires no row in `ActionAuthority`, because nothing about it is a caller's authority. `ladder.run` at LDR-093 is the governed action, and it is the only one this slice adds |

### Delivery, retry and confirmation

| Line | Fact |
|---|---|
| LDR-080 | Delivery goes through one port with one shape, so nothing above it knows which transport carried a message. FLR-08, FRD §19.2 G-06 |
| LDR-081 | The in-app channel is delivered by the row existing. Its confirmation moment is stamped when the row is written, in the same transaction as LDR-053 |
| LDR-082 | The email channel is delivered by a transport the administrator configures. FRD §14.1 puts connected systems and their credentials with the administrator, and CON-03 is satisfied because the mail server is the customer's own |
| LDR-083 | A recording transport is the default and is what runs in development: it writes what would have been sent onto the notification row and confirms it, and makes no network call. No message leaves this environment |
| LDR-084 | **The real transport cannot be click-verified here.** No mail server is reachable from this development environment, and every sample person's address is at `sample.invalid`, an RFC 2606 domain that can never route. The transport is built and typechecked and its behaviour against a real server is not proven, exactly as [[SLICE-01A]] records for the identity provider. Section 7 says which steps this makes unverifiable |
| LDR-085 | A failed delivery is retried with an increasing gap between attempts, up to a bounded number of attempts, after which the failure moment and the last error are recorded and the retrying stops. The specific bound and the gaps are the build's choice and are named in the final report, not invented in the plan |
| LDR-086 | Retrying never re-fires the rung. A retry is another attempt at delivering the row that already exists, so a person is never notified twice for one rung |
| LDR-087 | A failed delivery is visible to its recipient on GAP-SCR-010 as its own state, and is never rendered as delivered. `BR-DRV-09`'s reasoning applies here too: a channel that cannot confirm must never report success |
| LDR-088 | Nothing about a failed delivery removes the fired rung, its audit entry or the in-app row. The rung fired; only the message did not arrive |
| LDR-089 | An escalation that failed to deliver after every attempt has no firm-wide surface in this slice. Only the recipient sees it. That is weaker than FRD §11.3's reasoning about substantiating the claim to have escalated, and it is raised as an enhancement recommendation in section 8, unbuilt |

- **The personal queue.** `BR-ESC-03` names three surfaces for a fired rung: the record, notifications and the queue. SCR-010 and SCR-085 read the seed world entirely and belong to M-17. The queue gains fired rungs when it is wired.

- **The rest of SCR-049.** [[SLICE-17]] closes out the obligation detail. This slice adds one section and touches nothing else on the page.
