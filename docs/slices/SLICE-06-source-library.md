## 1 Outcome

The Source Library shows the firm's real legal instruments in the layout the
client approved, filtered, searched and scoped by the server, with every count on
the screen traceable to a record in the database.

---

## 2 Functional purpose

A compliance officer opens the Source Library to answer one question: which
bodies of law is this firm bound by, and which of them still have something
undecided sitting in them. FRD §8.2 describes the screen as the acts behind the
controls, sorted so items awaiting a decision rise to the top. It is the first
step of the headline flow in FRD §5.1: the system surfaces new or changed
instruments, and the officer picks the one that needs work.

What the screen accomplishes is triage. A statute is mostly machinery, and the
officer's time goes on the handful of provisions that create a duty. The library
does not show provisions; it shows which instrument holds an undecided one and
how many. The "Awaiting decision" figure and the amber "n awaiting" pill on each
row are the working surface. Everything else on the row exists so the officer can
recognise the instrument: its short title, its authority, its type and when it
was last updated.

The screen also carries the accountability boundary. FRD §4.5 and §5.2 step 4 put
a body of law in front of the departments it routes to, and FRD §4.10 makes
deciding that a body of law binds the firm the accountability of Compliance and
Company Secretarial alone. So the library a Finance and Tax user opens is not the
library a Compliance Manager opens, and only Compliance sees the control that
brings a new instrument in. FRD §4.9 offers the screen to the Executive, the Risk
Manager, the Compliance Manager, the Compliance Analyst and the Auditor, and to
nobody else through their own navigation.

### 8.1 The fiction on this screen

What works today only because the prototype's data was invented. Each is a
candidate delta.

| The fiction | Why it works in the prototype | What happens against real records |
