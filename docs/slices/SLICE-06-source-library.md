## 1 Outcome

The Source Library shows the firm's real legal instruments in the layout the
client approved, filtered, searched, counted and scoped by the server, with every
figure on the screen traceable to a record in the database.

---

## 2 Functional purpose

A compliance officer opens the Source Library to answer one question: which
bodies of law does this firm stand under, and which of them still hold something
nobody has decided about. FRD §8.2 describes the screen as the acts behind the
controls, sorted so items awaiting a decision rise to the top. It is step 1 of the
headline flow in FRD §5.1, where the system surfaces new or changed instruments
and the officer picks the one that needs work.

The work the screen supports is triage. A statute is mostly machinery, and the
officer's time goes on the handful of provisions that create a duty binding the
firm. The library never shows a provision. It shows which instrument holds an
undecided one and how many, so the officer picks an act rather than reads a
corpus. The "Awaiting decision" figure and the amber count on each row are the
working surface. The rest of the row exists so the officer can recognise the
instrument: its short title, the authority that issued it, its type and its date.

The screen also carries the accountability boundary. FRD §4.5 puts a record in
front of the department that owns it, FRD §5.2 step 4 has the person registering
an instrument assign the departments it routes to, and FRD §4.10 reserves the
judgement that a body of law binds the firm to Compliance and Company
Secretarial. So the library a Finance and Tax user opens is not the library a
Compliance Manager opens, and only Compliance sees the control that brings a new
instrument in. FRD §4.9 offers the screen to the Executive, the Risk Manager, the
Compliance Manager, the Compliance Analyst and the Auditor, and to nobody else
through their own navigation.

## 10 The slice loop discipline

Injected so it travels with the slice.

**One screen. One session. One commit.** If this slice needs two sessions it was
scoped too big. Part A and Part B are already separated for that reason.

**Plan before code.** Ask for the plan, read it, approve or redirect.

**Build only this slice's lines.** Anything else noticed goes in section 8. Never
built opportunistically.

**Point at the plan, never at the prototype source.** Say "implement SLICE-06
steps A1 to A4, contract lines SCR-053-030 to 042", not "build the sources screen
like the prototype". The second makes the model re-derive the screen from its own
understanding, and re-derivation drifts.

**Verify by clicking, side by side, personally.** Section 7 is the whole quality
control if you do not read code, and it cannot be delegated.

**Classify every difference.** Drift or delta. Nothing unclassified. Never call
something a delta because fixing it would be inconvenient.

**Report, do not resolve.** Ambiguity becomes a decision needed, with an example
and a recommendation. Never a guess.

**Never build an unapproved enhancement.** ER-012 and ER-013 both touch this
screen and neither is approved.

**Close out before starting the next slice.** An unclosed slice means the plan is
behind reality, and [[SLICE-07]] gets generated from a stale plan.
